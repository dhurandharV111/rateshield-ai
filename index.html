What the Fed does — plain English
What is the Federal Reserve?
The Fed controls the rate banks charge each other overnight. That one number ripples through every loan in the country within days.
Why raise rates?
When inflation is high, the Fed raises rates to make borrowing expensive. This slows spending and cools prices. CPI above 3% usually means hikes are coming.
Why cut rates?
When unemployment rises, the Fed cuts to encourage spending. Unemployment above 5% usually means cuts are coming.
How does this affect me?
Every adjustable loan moves with the Fed. Credit card rates rise the same day. Fixed rate loans never change — lock fixed when rates are low.
Key moments in Fed history
1981
Fed raised to 20% to crush 14.8% inflation. Mortgages hit 18%. Inflation dropped from 14% to 3% in 2 years.
2008
Financial crisis. Fed cut to 0.25% for 7 years. Best time in history to lock a fixed rate mortgage.
2022
Fastest hike in 40 years — 0.25% to 5.25% in 18 months. Credit cards jumped from 15% to 24%.
Now
Rates at 3.75% slowly easing. Window to refinance variable debt to fixed is open. Consider locking savings into a CD before rates fall further.
