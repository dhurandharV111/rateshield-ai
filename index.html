What the Fed actually does — plain English
What is the Federal Reserve?
The Fed is America's central bank. It controls the rate banks charge each other overnight. That one number ripples through every loan in the country within days.
Why raise rates?
When inflation is high, the Fed raises rates to make borrowing expensive. Expensive borrowing slows spending. Slower spending cools prices. CPI above 3% usually means hikes coming.
Why cut rates?
When unemployment rises, the Fed cuts rates to encourage borrowing and spending. Unemployment above 5% usually means cuts are coming.
How does this affect me?
Every adjustable loan moves with the Fed. Credit card rates rise same day. Fixed rate loans never change — always lock fixed when rates are low.
Key moments in Fed history
1981
Fed raised to 20% to crush 14.8% inflation. Mortgages hit 18%. Inflation dropped from 14% to 3% in 2 years.
2008
Financial crisis. Fed cut to 0.25% for 7 years. Mortgages hit record lows. Best time in history to lock a fixed rate.
2022
Fastest hike in 40 years — 0.25% to 5.25% in 18 months. Credit cards jumped from 15% to 24%. People without fixed mortgages paid dearly.
Now
Rates at 3.75% slowly easing. If you have variable debt, now is your window to refinance to fixed. If you have savings, consider locking into a CD before rates fall further.
